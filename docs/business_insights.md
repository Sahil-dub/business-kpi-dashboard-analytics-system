# Business Insights

The following findings are based on the generated KPI outputs in `outputs/`.

## Executive summary

- The business generated **$14.92M** in revenue and **$1.52M** in profit, for an overall **10.2% profit margin**.
- The customer base is highly retention-driven: **784 of 795 customers** placed repeat orders, which implies a **98.62% repeat customer rate** within the observed period.
- Growth is not evenly profitable across the catalog. Technology and Office Supplies support the business margin profile, while Furniture materially drags profitability.

## Revenue and trend observations

- Revenue peaked in **January 2009** at **$516.3K**, which also delivered the strongest monthly profit at **$62.3K**.
- The weakest revenue month in the dataset was **June 2011** at **$197.7K**.
- The most concerning month from a profitability perspective was **March 2010**, when revenue still reached **$217.8K** but profit fell to just **$1.1K**. That pattern suggests discounting, product mix, or cost pressure rather than demand collapse.
- Annual revenue declined after 2009 and only partially recovered by 2012, while annual profit also trended below the 2009 high. This suggests the business can still sell at scale, but mix quality weakened over time.

## Category and product performance

- **Technology** is the strongest category, contributing **$5.98M** in revenue and **$886.3K** in profit with a **14.81% margin**.
- **Office Supplies** delivered lower revenue than Technology but a healthy **13.8% margin**, making it an efficient profit engine.
- **Furniture** generated **$5.18M** in revenue but only **$117.4K** in profit, for a weak **2.27% margin**. This is the clearest category-level profitability problem.
- Within Furniture, **Tables** are the biggest issue: **$1.90M** in revenue but a **-$99.1K** profit contribution. **Bookcases** also underperform with **-$33.6K** profit.
- High-selling products are not always healthy. The `Polycom ViewStation ISDN Videoconferencing Unit` brought in **$255.3K** in revenue but still lost **$17.8K**, which makes it a good candidate for pricing or discount review.

## Customer behavior

- Average order value is **$2,713.90**, which is strong for a B2B-style retail catalog and supports an executive KPI card.
- Only **11 customers** appear to be one-time buyers across the full dataset window, reinforcing that the business relies heavily on existing-customer demand rather than one-off transactions.
- Corporate repeat customers generated the largest revenue pool at **$4.65M**, making that segment the most important to protect and expand.
- New-customer orders have higher average order values than repeat-customer orders in several segments, which suggests acquisition campaigns may be landing large first purchases even though the installed base drives most total revenue.

## Regional performance

- **Ontario** is the strongest geography, contributing **$3.06M** in revenue and **$346.9K** in profit across **1,235 orders**.
- The **West** region is commercially important across both British Columbia and Alberta, but its margins are weaker than Ontario and Prarie. That makes it a good candidate for freight, discount, and product-mix review.
- **Prarie / Saskachewan** stands out positively with a **12.61% margin**, outperforming several larger revenue regions on profitability quality.

## Recommended business actions

- Review Furniture pricing, discounting, and vendor economics first, especially for Tables and Bookcases.
- Protect Technology and Office Supplies as the primary margin drivers, but inspect high-revenue loss-making products before pushing them further.
- Build retention-focused reporting around Corporate customers because they anchor the largest repeat revenue stream.
- Add monthly anomaly review to management reporting so low-profit months like March 2010 are investigated quickly instead of being hidden by annual totals.
